Point of Equal Time (PET) — All Engines

fig_13_01Original redraw

The PET (critical point) is the point on track from which it takes the same time to continue to the destination as to return to the departure aerodrome. It depends only on the ground speeds on and home, not on fuel: distance to PET = D × H ÷ (O + H), where D is the total distance, H the ground speed home and O the ground speed on. In still air it is the mid-point; with wind it always moves into wind. For the all-engine case the normal cruise TAS is used for both O and H, and the time to reach the PET is found from the ground speed out.

Point of Equal Time (PET) — Engine Failure

fig_13_04Original redraw

Engine-failure PET — the same idea, planned for the loss of an engine. After the failure the aircraft cannot hold its cruise level: it drifts down to a lower stabilising level and flies at a lower TAS, whichever way it goes. So O and H are calculated with the engine-failure TAS and the winds at the lower level, which moves the PET. But the aircraft is still on all engines until it gets there, so the time (ETA) at the PET is calculated with the all-engine ground speed.

Point of Safe Return (PSR)

fig_14_01Original redraw

The PSR (point of no return) is the furthest point along track to which the aircraft can fly and still return to the departure aerodrome with the required reserves intact. It matters when the destination has no usable alternate. Unlike the PET it depends on fuel: time to PSR = E × H ÷ (O + H), where E is the safe endurance, O the ground speed out and H the ground speed home. The distance out equals the distance home. Any wind component, head or tail, reduces the distance to the PSR compared with still air.
